Because we have designed Screens 4 through 9 to automatically handle 99% of Wadaan’s daily business (buying cement, paying contractors, receiving installments), you will rarely use Screen 2.
Think of the General Journal as your specialized "Accountant’s Tool." It is used strictly for manual, non-standard money movements that don't belong on a standard bill or customer receipt.
1. What You See (The Interface)
The screen looks like a classic, professional accounting ledger designed for precise adjustments.
The Header: At the top, there is a field for the Date, a space for a detailed Description (e.g., "Owner taking cash for personal use"), and an auto-generated Journal Voucher Number (e.g., JV-0042) to keep a perfect paper trail.
The Entry Grid: Below the header is a table with at least two empty rows. Each row has:
A dropdown to select an Account (from Screen 1).
A Debit column (Money going in / increasing an asset/expense).
A Credit column (Money going out / increasing liability/revenue).
The Math Footer: At the absolute bottom, there are live totals summing up all your Debits and all your Credits, along with a "Difference" indicator.
2. When to Use This Screen (Real-World Scenarios)
As the solo operator, you will only open Screen 2 for very specific situations:
Owner Withdrawals: If you take Rs. 100,000 out of the Meezan Bank account for personal family expenses, there is no "Supplier Bill" for that. You use this screen to manually move Rs. 100k from the Bank bucket to your "Owner Drawings" equity bucket.
Fixing Mistakes: If you accidentally paid a Rs. 15,000 bill last month and categorized it as "Office Rent" instead of "Marketing," you use this screen to move the Rs. 15,000 to the correct bucket without having to delete old, closed bills.
Starting the Software (Opening Balances): On day one of using this ERP, you will use this screen to tell the system exactly how much cash is currently sitting in your real-world bank accounts and office safe to get the system running.
3. The "Two-Line" Minimum Rule
Accounting is based on the law of physics: money cannot just appear or disappear; it must travel. Because of this, you can never fill out just one row on this screen. If you reduce the Office Safe by Rs. 50,000, you must tell the system exactly where that Rs. 50,000 went on the second row. Every entry requires at least two lines to tell the full story.
4. Business Rules & Guardrails
This screen is powerful, so it comes with strict, built-in safety nets to ensure you never accidentally break Wadaan’s accounting.
The Golden Rule (The Zero-Balance Lock): This is the most important rule in the entire ERP. The total at the bottom of the Debit column must exactly equal the total in the Credit column. If Debits are Rs. 100,000 and Credits are Rs. 99,999, the "Post" button physically disables itself. The system will absolutely refuse to save until the math is perfectly balanced.
The "Hands-Off" System Lock: The system will block you from manually adjusting certain system-locked accounts here, like the Accounts Payable bucket. Why? Because if you manually reduce Accounts Payable here, the system won't know which specific Vendor's (e.g., Ali Hardware) debt you reduced. To adjust Vendor or Customer debt, you are forced to use the proper Bill (Screen 5) or Receipt (Screen 9) screens so the Khaatas stay accurate.
Screen 2 is your safety valve for unique financial moves, keeping Wadaan's books legally and mathematically sound.
